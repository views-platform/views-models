
\textbf{MSE} --- ordinary squared error, every cell-month weighted equally. The library's own
implementation, a thin wrapper around PyTorch's. Used by \texttt{dark\_river}, \texttt{silent\_fox}
and \texttt{red\_hawk}.

\textbf{Spotlight} (\texttt{SpotlightLossLogcosh}) --- in-house, not off the shelf. A three-part
objective computed after an \texttt{asinh} transform. It scores the \textit{shape} of the error only
in cells where the observation or the forecast exceeds one fatality; separately penalises the
\textit{level} in cells whose observation exceeds one fatality; and separately penalises
forecasting conflict in cells that stayed below it. It uses \texttt{log cosh} rather than squared
error, so a single very large observation does not dominate the gradient. Used by the other six.

\subsection*{What You Should Know About Spotlight}

It is an in-house construction (author: Dylan), developed since March 2026; the variant used here dates from April
and is the fifth iteration of a family of related objectives. He has written a detailed account of
how it works. One of its two parameters is derived rather than tuned --- the threshold separating
``event'' from ``quiet'' cells is $\mathrm{asinh}(1) \approx 0.88$, exactly one fatality on the
transformed scale. The other, a weight on a spectral term, was chosen by hyperparameter sweep.

\textbf{What does not exist is any verification of it.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No citation.} I searched the source files, their documentation, the commit
          history and the architecture decision records. Two of the other objectives in that same
          in-house library do carry references to published papers, so this is not a codebase that
          never cites anything.
    \item \textbf{No tests.} The library's test suite covers eight of its objectives and this is
          not among them. Nothing checks its gradients, nothing compares it against a reference
          implementation, and nothing establishes that the code computes what the written account
          says it computes.
\end{itemize}

That verification belongs with the author in the first instance. Tests pinning the gradients and a
derivation someone else can follow line by line are what make a bespoke objective reviewable;
absent those, an outside review means reconstructing the intended design before you can judge the
built one. I have not spent my time that way, and I think the order matters: tests and derivation
first, review after. So I am not in a position to vouch for this objective, and I would rather say
so than imply an assurance I have not earned.

\textbf{In practice: it could be excellent, adequate, or quietly wrong, and nothing available to me
distinguishes those three.} That bears on how much weight a comparison between the Spotlight models
and the three MSE ones can carry.
