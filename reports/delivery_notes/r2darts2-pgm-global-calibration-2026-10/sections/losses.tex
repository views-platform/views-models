
\textbf{MSE} --- ordinary squared error, every cell-month weighted equally. The library's own
implementation, a thin wrapper around PyTorch's. Used by \texttt{dark\_river}, \texttt{silent\_fox}
and \texttt{red\_hawk}.

\textbf{Spotlight} (\texttt{SpotlightLossLogcosh}) --- in-house, not off the shelf. A three-part
objective computed after an \texttt{asinh} transform. It scores the \textit{shape} of the error only
in cells where the observation or the forecast exceeds one fatality; separately penalises the
\textit{level} in cells whose observation exceeds one fatality; and separately penalises
forecasting conflict in cells that stayed below it. It uses \texttt{log cosh} rather than squared
error, so a single very large observation does not dominate the gradient. Used by the other six.

\subsection*{Development and Validation Status}

Spotlight is not a one-off function but the production member of a sustained line of work. The
first variant was committed in March 2026 and the one used here at the end of April; development
continued into late August. The family now comprises six Spotlight variants and three related
objectives --- roughly 2,250 lines across nine modules --- attracting some 216 commits over six
months. The library's own documentation sets out five earlier generations of the design, recording
for each what it addressed and what it exposed. By any ordinary measure this represents a
considerable investment of development effort.

The objective takes a single parameter, and it is derived rather than fitted: the threshold
separating event from quiet cells is $\mathrm{asinh}(1) \approx 0.88$, exactly one fatality on the
transformed scale.

What the record does not contain is any formal account of it. No citation appears in the source,
its documentation, the commit history or the architecture decision records; two other objectives in
the same library do cite published work, so the omission is particular to this family rather than a
habit of the codebase. Neither is it covered by tests. The library's suite exercises eight of its
objectives and this is not among them, so nothing checks its gradients, compares it against a
reference implementation, or establishes that the code computes what the written account describes
--- this for the objective on which 37 of the platform's 42 darts models are trained.

Six months of concentrated development across five successive generations is not the profile of an
untried design, and the written account of its construction is detailed. It is reasonable to infer
that the objective has been exercised extensively, if informally, during that period. Informal
exercise is not validation, however, and the distinction is what this note is obliged to record:
what is absent is not the work but the evidence of it. A derivation a third party can follow, and
tests that fail when the implementation is wrong, remain outstanding and should be produced for an
objective in production use at this scale.

On that basis this note takes no position on whether the objective is sound. It may be excellent,
adequate, or quietly wrong, and nothing in the available record distinguishes those possibilities.
Six of the nine delivered models were trained on it and three on ordinary squared error, which
bears on how much weight a comparison between those two groups can carry.
